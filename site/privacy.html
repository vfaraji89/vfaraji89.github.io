Both sites are static pages served by GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA). Like any web server, GitHub receives your IP address and browser details when you open a page and may keep them in its logs for security. I have no access to those logs. See GitHub’s privacy statement.

Analytics

With your consent, Google Analytics 4 (Google Ireland Ltd. and Google LLC) counts visits and a few actions, such as opening the CV, proposing a call or following a link to GitHub, so I can see which pages help people.

  • Only after a yes. The Google Analytics script is not loaded until you choose “Sure”. If you choose “No”, or say nothing, Google receives nothing from these pages.
  • Kept lean. Advertising storage and ad personalisation are always off. Before an event is sent, email addresses and phone numbers are removed, values are cut to 100 characters, and text you type or paste is never sent: only counts and scores.
  • Cookies. Google sets _ga and _ga_<id>, which last up to two years. Google Analytics keeps event data for at most 14 months.
  • Legal basis. Your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a); KVKK Art. 5(1), explicit consent). You can withdraw it at any time; a “No” also deletes the cookies already set.
  • Transfer. Google may process the data in the USA. Google LLC is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. See Google’s privacy policy.

Stored in your browser

A few settings are kept in your browser’s storage so the site remembers what you chose. They never leave your device and are not cookies:

  • vf-lang: your language.
  • vf-consent: your analytics answer.
  • vf-recruiter, vf-sound: the recruiter view and sound switches.
  • pop-fit (for this visit only): whether the Fit check hint was shown.

You can delete them at any time in your browser’s site settings.

Tools that run in your browser

The Fit check (paste a job description), the Role check on the guide (paste your CV) and the corner agent (ask a question) all work inside your browser, with code and data that come with the page. What you paste or type is not sent to me, to Google or to anyone else, and it is gone when you close the page. If you allowed analytics, only numbers are counted, such as how many requirements were found and the score.

When you contact me

  • Suggest a time. The booking form sends your name, email, the date and time you propose, your time zone and an optional topic to my Google account (through a Google Apps Script) so I can reply.
  • Calendar link, email, LinkedIn. Google Calendar’s booking page, email and LinkedIn are run by those companies, under their own privacy terms. I receive what you choose to send.

Why: to answer you and arrange a conversation. Legal basis: your request and the steps before a possible collaboration, and my legitimate interest in replying (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b) and (f); KVKK Art. 5(2)(c) and (f)). How long: I delete booking requests and our correspondence within 12 months of our last exchange, unless we work together or the law requires me to keep them longer.

The job market tracker

The guide’s market pages count what applied AI job postings ask for. Once a day a script reads public postings from company career boards (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday) and public listings (Remotive, Arbeitnow and the Hacker News “Who is hiring?” thread).

  • For each posting it keeps only a scrambled (hashed) id, the job title in a normalised form, the role family, the level, the skills and tools it names, whether it is remote, and the dates it was first and last seen.
  • It keeps no names of people, no company names, no links and no posting text. The text is read only while the script runs, to classify the posting, and is then discarded.
  • Results are published only as totals and shares. Hacker News posts are written by people; nothing that identifies them is kept.

Legal basis: my legitimate interest in publishing educational statistics about the job market (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f); KVKK Art. 5(2)(f)).

The tool map and weekly discovery

The guide lists software tools from public sources: official websites, GitHub and Hacker News. A weekly script looks for new tools and refreshes GitHub star counts. Public repository details (name, owner, link, stars, dates) can name a person when a repository belongs to an individual developer. Only public facts about the software are kept. If you want a tool or your name removed, write to vfaraji89@gmail.com and it will be. Legal basis: legitimate interest in an accurate, educational map of the field.
